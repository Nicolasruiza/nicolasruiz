Featured case study · iCRM

From a legacy global CRM to a product countries wanted to adopt

The global iCRM platform had become monolithic and legacy, and countries across Latin America were increasingly unhappy with the experience. Chile had stopped using it altogether and moved to an application created by its Analytics team. Technical debt made even small improvements painfully slow — a basic back-button change could take eight months.

$3MInvestment from Scotiabank Chile
Top 5Recognized among the bank's most agile projects
LATAMRegional roadshow and reusable delivery practices

Challenge

A global CRM had accumulated enough technical debt and experience problems that countries were losing confidence in it; Chile had already abandoned the platform for a locally built alternative.

Initiative

On my own time, I built a web prototype using the company's newer design library and reimagined the CRM around a Customer 360, cross-channel campaigns, and analytics-driven insights such as next-best-action.

From prototype to product

The concept gained internal traction, a dedicated team was formed, and we took the vision on a roadshow across Latin America. Scotiabank Chile backed it with a $3M investment and became the first implementation.

Agile operating model

I led the team's Scrum and Agile practices and made them repeatable by documenting the ways of working, ceremonies, and delivery patterns. Those practices became a reference case and were reused by other projects across the bank. The team was recognized as one of Scotiabank's top 5 most agile projects.

Outcome

What started as a self-initiated prototype became a funded product, implemented first in Chile and now expanding to additional countries. The delivery model also scaled: the Agile practices I led were documented, reused by other teams, and recognized among the bank's strongest examples of Agile execution.

Featured case study · Technisys

Turning a waterfall-heavy implementation model into a repeatable delivery playbook

As Technisys expanded from Latin America into North America following new funding from California, the company grew rapidly from roughly 500 employees to more than 1,500. That growth exposed a major scaling problem: client implementations were still heavily waterfall, discovery could take five months of documentation, role overlap created redundancy, and post-implementation satisfaction was not where the company wanted it to be.

Operating ModelDiscoveryAgile TransformationImplementation PlaybookEstimationCross-functional Alignment
5 mo → 2–3 wkDiscovery cycle reduced across the implementation model
90%Satisfaction on the pilot project
500 → 1,500+Company growth the model needed to support

Challenge

The company needed a scalable way to implement its core banking product with new clients. Despite attempts to work Agile, implementations remained largely waterfall, discoveries could consume five months in paperwork, responsibilities overlapped across roles, and the client experience after implementation was inconsistent.

Operating model

I helped streamline overlapping roles and redesigned the discovery dynamic around a documented implementation playbook. Instead of prescribing every ceremony, the model gave teams freedom in how they worked while defining clear, tangible outcomes that every discovery had to produce.

Repeatable execution

Project managers could follow a common template and supporting documentation rather than reinventing the implementation approach for every client. The structure made the method reusable while preserving enough flexibility for different project contexts.

Aligned estimation

We also created an automated estimation calculator shared across Product, Project, and Sales so commercial estimates and delivery plans started from the same assumptions, reducing the risk of time and cost deviations later in the implementation.

Outcome

The pilot project reached 90% satisfaction, discovery dropped from roughly five months to about two to three weeks, and the documented playbook created a repeatable implementation model that project managers could apply as the company continued scaling.

Featured case study · Data & Analytics

Proving International Banking data could be delivered — then scaling the model across LATAM

Data & Analytics had never successfully obtained and operationalized International Banking data. Multiple previous attempts had failed, and our team initially ran into the same barriers. We changed the operating model early: instead of waiting for a large end-to-end delivery, we worked incrementally and produced visible proof that data could be obtained, processed, and presented. That early proof changed the relationship with the countries because this no longer looked like another attempt that might disappear.

Data ProductIncremental DeliveryCloud MigrationOperating ModelAnalyticsExecutive KPIs
$47MExpense-reduction opportunities identified from the data
12 → 50+Team growth as the model expanded across LATAM
CloudOne of the early on-prem-to-cloud use cases standardized in the department

Challenge

International Banking data had resisted repeated attempts to centralize it. The technical and organizational barriers were significant, confidence from the countries was low, and the business had never had a reliable stream of data products from the region.

Incremental proof

We designed an incremental delivery model that generated early proof of value: obtain a slice of data, make it usable, present it, and build confidence before expanding further. Those tangible wins improved country participation and gave the program credibility.

Scale & standardization

We optimized the end-to-end process and delivered enough dashboards and data products that the business created new departments to absorb and use the information. The operating model became a reference practice in Data & Analytics, and the program expanded from a 12-person team to more than 50 people across Latin America.

Business insight

The new data exposed material opportunities, including customer segments at the bottom of the portfolio that were generating more cost than value for the bank. Those insights enabled targeted optimization and contributed to identifying $47M in expense-reduction opportunities.

Outcome

The initiative became one of the department's early examples of moving from on-premise data to cloud and helped establish a standardized delivery practice. It also created the KPI framework used by stakeholders to present BNS results to the market, while turning previously inaccessible International Banking data into an ongoing source of operational and financial insight.
